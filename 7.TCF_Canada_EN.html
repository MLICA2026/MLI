The exam our students are preparing for: the four tests, the tasks, and what each one checks.

🧭 The four tests

TCF Canada has four mandatory tests. Results are converted to NCLC / CLB levels, which is what students need for immigration.

🎧 CO · Listening

39 questions · 35 minutes.

  • Multiple choice: 4 options (A, B, C, D), only one is correct.
  • Questions get progressively harder.
  • A sound signal plays before each audio document.
  • Each recording and its question are heard only once.

Strategies to teach

Most useful from question 20 onward, where the recordings get longer.

  1. Understand the question first. It tells the student what to listen for.
  2. Catch key words. If the whole recording is not understood, focus on words or phrases that are repeated: they usually carry the main idea.
  3. Listen for opinion markers to identify the speaker's attitude: je pense que, je regrette, à mon avis, heureusement, malheureusement.
  4. Focus on the ending. Speakers often conclude or state their position in the last sentences. Signal words: en fait, au final, en conclusion, c'est pourquoi, cela montre que. A student who lost the beginning can often still answer from the end.
  5. Eliminate. Cross out options that clearly do not match what was heard, then choose among the rest.
  6. Infer. The answer is often not stated word for word. The speaker describes a situation or a trend, and the correct option is its summary or its consequence.
Example of inference

A speaker explains that since her town opened bike lanes she no longer takes the car to work, and that several colleagues do the same. No one says "the bike lanes changed people's habits", but that is the option to choose.

Understanding over memorising

Students should not rely on memorised answers: the same recording can come with different options. Train comprehension of the content.

What to train in class

Reading the written instructions and options before the audio starts, answering on a single listen, and moving on without dwelling on a missed question.

📖 CE · Reading

39 questions · 60 minutes.

  • Multiple choice: 4 options (A, B, C, D), only one is correct.
  • Questions get progressively harder.
  • Time management is the main difficulty: students who hesitate too long should move to the next question.

Strategies to teach

  1. Question first, then the text. Read with the question in mind and look for its answer.
  2. Read the first 15–20 questions closely, sentence by sentence, paying attention to logical links: cause, contrast, time order.
  3. Find the key sentence. The first or last sentence of a paragraph usually carries its main idea.
  4. Expect paraphrase. The correct option rarely repeats the words of the text; it says the same thing differently.
  5. Eliminate. Remove options that are too extreme, off-topic, or not supported by the text, then pick the one that fits the overall meaning.
  6. Identify the author's attitude (positive, negative, neutral, critical) and discard options that go against it.
  7. Watch contrast words: mais, cependant, pourtant, toutefois. What follows is often what the question is about.
  8. Be wary of absolutes: options with tout, tous, toujours, jamais are rarely correct.
  9. Keep moving. Never stay too long on one sentence or one paragraph.
Example: paraphrase

Text: « Depuis qu'elle travaille à domicile, Julie gagne deux heures par jour qu'elle passait auparavant dans les transports. »
Correct option: « Le télétravail lui fait économiser du temps. » Same idea, different words.

Example: contrast and absolutes

Text: « Les réseaux sociaux permettent de garder le contact avec ses proches, mais ils peuvent aussi isoler certains utilisateurs. »
Correct: « Ils sont utiles mais comportent des risques. » Wrong: « Ils isolent toujours leurs utilisateurs. » The text says certains, not all.

✍️ EE · Writing

3 tasks · 60 minutes.

TaskWhat the student writesWords
Task 1A message to one or several recipients (status given in the instruction) to describe, tell and/or explain.60–120
Task 2An article, letter or note for several readers: an account of an experience or a story, with comments, opinions or arguments, according to a goal (e.g. make a claim, reconcile).120–150
Task 3A text (for a newspaper, website, colleague, manager…) comparing two points of view on a social issue, taken from two short documents of about 90 words each, then the student's own position.120–180

Suggested structure

Task 1 · message

  1. Subject line (e.g. Mes congés à Marrakech).
  2. Greeting.
  3. Purpose of the message.
  4. Details: who, what, when, where, with whom.
  5. Concrete expectations, depending on the instruction.
  6. Recommendation, promise or thanks, then a closing formula (À bientôt, Amicalement, Cordialement…).

Task 2 · article / account

  1. A catchy title.
  2. First paragraph: a short, engaging presentation of the topic or activity.
  3. The student's own experience, with details.
  4. Recommendations to the readers, depending on the instruction.

Task 3 · two points of view

  1. Title.
  2. Part 1 (40–60 words): summarise the point of view of each document.
  3. Part 2 (80–120 words): the student's own point of view, argued.

What is assessed

  • Communicating a message clearly and giving the requested information.
  • Describing, telling, explaining.
  • Justifying a choice, a position, a decision.
  • Linking ideas coherently.
  • Comparing two points of view.
  • Giving and arguing an opinion.
  • Using vocabulary and structures suited to the task.
  • Summarising and reformulating.
Word counts

Train students to stay inside the minimum and maximum for each task, and to split their 60 minutes across the three tasks.

🎤 EO · Speaking

3 tasks · 12 minutes in total, as a face-to-face interview with an examiner.

TaskFormatTimeWhat it checks
Task 1Guided interview, no preparation2 minCan the candidate talk with someone they do not know?
Task 2Interaction, with preparation. The candidate asks the questions and leads the conversation.5 min 30Can the candidate obtain specific information in an everyday situation?
Task 3Expressing a point of view, no preparation4 min 30Can the candidate speak spontaneously, continuously and convincingly?

Example prompts

Task 1

  • Quel est votre film préféré ? Pourquoi ?
  • Où êtes-vous allé durant vos dernières vacances ?
  • Comment imaginez-vous votre vie dans 30 ans ?
  • Où avez-vous appris le français ?

Task 2

  • Je dirige une association d'aide aux personnes en difficulté. Demandez-moi comment fonctionne cette association (actions, financements, adhérents…).
  • Vous partez en vacances et vous recherchez quelqu'un pour garder votre chat. Je vous propose ma candidature. Posez-moi des questions pour décider si je suis capable de garder votre chat.
  • Vous vous interrogez sur l'organisation des transports en commun en France. Posez-moi toutes les questions à ce sujet (fréquence, horaires, tarifs…).

Task 3

  • Faut-il à votre avis interdire la vente d'alcool aux mineurs ?
  • Voyager nous rend meilleurs. Êtes-vous d'accord avec cette affirmation ? Pourquoi ?
  • Les moyens de communication ne cessent de se développer. Les gens communiquent-ils mieux aujourd'hui ?
What to train in class

Task 2 is the one students find least natural: they must ask, not answer. Practise building 8–10 varied questions during the preparation time.
